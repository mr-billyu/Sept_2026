How God Talks to Us

The Church teaches that God communicates in many ways, often subtle and interior. Here are some of the main ways to recognize His voice:

1. Through Scripture (the Bible).

  • The Word of God is living and active (Heb 4:12). When you read Scripture prayerfully, certain passages can seem to speak directly to your life, your struggles, or your hopes. This is often how God makes His will known.

2. Through the Church.

  • The Church is guided by the Holy Spirit. The teachings, sacraments, and liturgy are channels where God reveals Himself and nourishes us.

3. Through Prayer.

  • Prayer isn't just us talking---it's also listening. In quiet moments of prayer or Eucharistic Adoration, you may sense guidance, peace, or conviction that points to God's voice.

4. Through Conscience.

  • The Catechism (CCC 1776) calls conscience the "inner sanctuary" where we hear God's law written on our hearts. When you discern right from wrong, that inner tug is often God speaking.

5. Through Circumstances and People.

  • God can use events in our lives, the counsel of wise friends, or even difficulties to communicate His will.

6. Through Silence.

  • Sometimes God's "voice" is more about presence than words---an assurance of being loved and guided even without clear answers.

Saints often warn that we need humility and discernment, since not every thought or feeling is from God. That's why Catholics lean on spiritual direction, the wisdom of the Church, and consistent prayer.

St. Teresa of Ávila (1515--1582)

  • She was a Carmelite nun and mystic who wrote about prayer in The Interior Castle.
  • Teresa described different "ways" God communicates in prayer: sometimes through thoughts and inspirations, other times through deep inner peace or love that words can't explain.
  • She warned that not all interior voices are from God, so humility, obedience, and discernment are essential.

St. Ignatius of Loyola (1491--1556)

  • Founder of the Jesuits, known for his Spiritual Exercises.

  • He taught about "discernment of spirits":

    • Movements that draw you toward God (peace, love, deeper faith) are from the Holy Spirit.
    • Movements that draw you away from God (confusion, despair, pride) are not.

  • Ignatius said God often speaks through our desires, feelings, and inner movements when we pay close attention.

St. John of the Cross (1542--1591)

  • He emphasized that God's deepest communication is often wordless---a silence or darkness of faith where God's love transforms the soul.
  • He warned against chasing "visions" or "voices" and instead trusted God's presence in simplicity and faith.

St. Augustine (354--430)

  • In his Confessions, Augustine recounts hearing a childlike voice say, "Take and read", which led him to open the Bible and find the verse that changed his life.
  • For him, Scripture was the primary way God spoke directly into his situation.
