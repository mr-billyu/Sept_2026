Knowing versus Living

In Catholic teaching, there is a profound difference between knowing the faith and living the faith. We are called to both.

Knowing the Faith

Knowing the faith means learning what God has revealed:

  • Reading the RSV-CE Bible.
  • Studying the Catechism of the Catholic Church.
  • Learning the teachings of the Church.
  • Understanding the lives of the saints and Sacred Tradition.

Knowledge is essential because Jesus said:

"If you continue in my word, you are truly my disciples." (John 8:31, RSV-CE)

The Catechism teaches:

"Faith seeks understanding." (CCC 158)

Living the Faith

Living the faith means allowing that knowledge to transform your life:

  • Loving God above all things.
  • Loving your neighbor.
  • Praying daily.
  • Receiving the sacraments worthily.
  • Forgiving others.
  • Carrying your cross.
  • Practicing humility, charity, patience, and obedience.
  • Trusting in Divine Providence.

Jesus warns that knowledge alone is not enough:

"Not every one who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of my Father." (Matthew 7:21, RSV-CE)

St. James writes:

"Be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves." (James 1:22, RSV-CE)

The saints consistently emphasized this. St. Francis de Sales said:

"Knowledge without practice serves only to make us more culpable."

A simple way to think of it is:

  • Knowing is like studying a map.
  • Living is actually walking the road.

The goal of the Christian life is not merely to know about Christ but to become like Him. Every truth we learn should lead us to greater love of God and neighbor. As Jesus said:

"If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them." (John 13:17, RSV-CE)

Knowledge lights the path, but obedience, love, and grace carry us along it. The mature Catholic life unites both: an informed mind and a transformed heart.
