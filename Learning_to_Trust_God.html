Learning to Trust God Better

A Catholic Cheat Sheet

The basic problem: distrust

The Catechism describes the first sin as beginning with a loss of trust in God:

"Man, tempted by the devil, let his trust in his Creator die in his
heart..."
--- Catechism of the Catholic Church, 397

Original sin leaves human nature wounded. We are therefore prone to fear, self-reliance, suspicion of God's goodness, and the desire to control things ourselves. The Christian life is, in an important sense, a gradual restoration of our trust in the Father.

The goal is not to manufacture a feeling of trust. It is to choose to trust God, with His grace, even when our feelings are afraid.

A Simple Daily Path to Greater Trust

1. Tell God honestly where you do not trust Him

Don't pretend the fear isn't there. Bring it directly to Him.

Prayer:
Father, I am afraid about ________. I know that I am having trouble trusting You with this. Help me to trust You.

Remember the prayer in Mark 9:24:

"I believe; help my unbelief!"

We can similarly pray: "Lord, I trust You; help my distrust."

2. Remember who God is

Trust grows when we know the One we are trusting.

Ask yourself:

  • Is God my Father?
  • Does He love me?
  • Is He good?
  • Does He know what I need?
  • Is He wiser than I am?
  • Is He with me even when I cannot understand what He is doing?

Jesus repeatedly teaches us to look upon God as our Father.

Matthew 6:31--32 --- Our heavenly Father knows what we need.

Matthew 7:11 --- The Father gives good things to those who ask Him.

Luke 12:32 --- "Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom."

3. Practice trust with one concrete thing

Don't try to surrender your entire life all at once.

Choose one thing that is worrying you today.

Say:

"Jesus, I give this particular thing to You. I will do what I reasonably can, and I entrust what I cannot control to You."

Then, when the worry returns, give the same thing back to Him again.

You may have to do this twenty times. That is not failure. That is practice.

4. Look backward and remember

One of the best ways to trust God with tomorrow is to remember His faithfulness yesterday.

At the end of the day ask:

"Where did I see God's care today?"

It may be something very small:

  • a prayer answered,
  • help arriving at the right moment,
  • strength to endure something difficult,
  • another person's kindness,
  • an unexpected peace,
  • or simply being carried through a day you were worried about.

Remembering God's past faithfulness trains the heart to trust His future providence.

5. Receive God's grace in the Sacraments

Trust is not something we build entirely by our own effort.

In Confession, we experience the Father's mercy after we have failed.

In the Eucharist, Jesus gives us Himself.

The Sacraments teach something deeper than an idea:

God has not abandoned me. He comes to me.

6. When you cannot understand God, trust His character

Sometimes the hardest prayer is:

"Father, I do not understand this, but I believe that You are good."

Trusting God does not mean trusting that everything will turn out as I want. It means trusting that whatever happens, God will remain my Father, He will not abandon me, and He can bring me through even what I cannot understand.

Trust does not always remove the cross. Sometimes trust is what carries us through it.

Trust does not mean believing that everything that happens is good.

It means believing that God remains good even in circumstances we do not understand, and that He can bring good even out of suffering.

Romans 8:28 is especially important here: God works in everything for good with those who love Him.

A Very Short "Trust Prayer"

When fear, worry, or the desire to control something appears, use this:

Father, You love me.
You know what I need.
You know what I cannot see.
I will do what You ask of me today.
What I cannot control, I place in Your hands.
Jesus, I trust in You.

A 5-Minute Evening Examination of Trust

Before bed:

  1. Thank God for one good thing from the day.
  2. Ask: Where did I trust God today?
  3. Ask: Where did fear or self-reliance take over?
  4. Give that situation to God.
  5. Finish with: "Father, into Your hands I place tomorrow."

This is not an examination designed to make us feel guilty.

It is a way of learning trust through repetition.

When You Catch Yourself Worrying

Try this simple sequence:

NOTICE → NAME → SURRENDER → ACT

NOTICE
"I am worrying."

NAME
"I am afraid that ________."

SURRENDER
"Jesus, I give this to You."

ACT
"What is the one reasonable thing God wants me to do right now?"

Then do that thing and leave the rest with Him.

Scripture for Learning Trust

A few passages worth returning to frequently:

  • Proverbs 3:5--6 --- Trust in the Lord rather than relying entirely on your own understanding.
  • Psalm 23 --- The Lord is my shepherd.
  • Psalm 37:5 --- Commit your way to the Lord and trust Him.
  • Psalm 56:3--4 --- When afraid, place your trust in God.
  • Isaiah 26:3--4 --- Trust in the Lord forever.
  • Matthew 6:25--34 --- Jesus teaches us not to be anxious about tomorrow.
  • Matthew 7:7--11 --- The goodness of our heavenly Father.
  • Mark 9:24 --- "I believe; help my unbelief!"
  • Luke 12:22--32 --- "Fear not, little flock."
  • John 14:1 --- "Let not your hearts be troubled; believe in God, believe also in me."
  • Romans 8:28 --- God works for good with those who love Him.
  • Romans 8:31--39 --- Nothing can separate us from the love of God.
  • 1 Peter 5:7 --- Cast your anxieties upon Him because He cares for you.

Saints Who Can Teach Us Trust

St. Thérèse of Lisieux

Her "Little Way" is deeply connected with childlike confidence in the Father. She teaches us that holiness is not primarily accomplishing extraordinary things, but allowing ourselves to depend upon God's mercy like a little child.

St. Faustina Kowalska

The Divine Mercy devotion centers on the simple prayer:

Jesus, I trust in You.

Her writings repeatedly return to confidence in God's mercy, particularly when we are conscious of our weakness.

St. Francis de Sales

He strongly teaches peaceful confidence in God's providence: faithfully perform the duty God has placed before you today rather than becoming consumed by what might happen tomorrow.

The Main Idea

The opposite of the first temptation is not merely obedience to a rule.

The serpent's temptation suggested:

"Perhaps God isn't really good. Perhaps you cannot trust Him."

The Christian response becomes:

"God is my Father. He is good. He loves me. Therefore I can entrust myself to Him."

Trust usually does not appear all at once.

It grows through hundreds of little acts of surrender.

And perhaps one of the simplest prayers for that entire journey is:

Jesus, I trust in You.

And when I don't trust You very well, teach me how.
