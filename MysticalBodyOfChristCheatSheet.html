THE MYSTICAL BODY OF CHRIST

Living and Acting Like Christ — A Catholic Cheat Sheet

“Now you are the body of Christ and individually members of it.”
— 1 Corinthians 12:27

1. THE BIG IDEA

When we are baptized into Christ, Christianity does not simply mean:

“Jesus lived a good life. I should try to copy Him.”

Something much deeper has happened.

Through Baptism we have been united to Christ. Christ is the Head, and we are members of His Body, the Church.

Jesus continues His work in the world through His Mystical Body.

That means:

  • Christ loves through His members.
  • Christ comforts through His members.
  • Christ teaches through His members.
  • Christ feeds the hungry through His members.
  • Christ visits the lonely through His members.
  • Christ forgives through His members.
  • Christ prays through His members.
  • Christ cares for the sick through His members.
  • Christ raises families through His members.
  • Christ performs countless hidden acts of love through His members.

We are not Christ Himself, and our actions remain genuinely our own.

But through grace we are united to Him, and He can work in us and through us.

A useful question therefore becomes:

Jesus, what do You want to do through me today?

2. ONE BODY — MANY MEMBERS

One of the most important things to understand about the Mystical Body is that God does not expect every member to perform every function.

St. Paul compares the Church to a human body.

The eye is not the hand.

The hand is not the foot.

The ear is not the eye.

Yet all belong to the same body.

See especially 1 Corinthians 12:12–27.

The same principle applies to Christians.

One person may be especially drawn toward:

  • teaching,
  • caring for the sick,
  • helping the poor,
  • encouraging discouraged people,
  • prayer and intercession,
  • hospitality,
  • evangelization,
  • raising children,
  • caring for elderly relatives,
  • parish service,
  • practical repair work,
  • music,
  • sacred art,
  • defending human dignity,
  • comforting the grieving,
  • intellectual work,
  • financial generosity,
  • quiet hidden service.

Another Catholic may serve Christ in very different ways.

This diversity is not a defect.

It is part of God's design for the Body.

3. TWO DIFFERENT QUESTIONS

This distinction is extremely important.

Question 1: What must EVERY Christian become?

Every Christian is called to become:

  • loving,
  • holy,
  • merciful,
  • forgiving,
  • humble,
  • truthful,
  • patient,
  • faithful,
  • prayerful,
  • just,
  • generous,
  • obedient to God,
  • pure of heart,
  • compassionate,
  • willing to sacrifice,
  • concerned for his neighbor.

These are not optional specialties.

We cannot say:

“Forgiveness isn't my gift.”

or:

“Loving my neighbor isn't my vocation.”

These belong to the Christian life itself.

Question 2: What particular things is GOD asking ME to do?

That answer will differ from person to person.

God gives us different:

  • vocations,
  • circumstances,
  • responsibilities,
  • abilities,
  • opportunities,
  • personalities,
  • relationships,
  • charisms,
  • resources,
  • stages of life.

Therefore:

Everyone is called to become like Christ.

Not everyone is called to perform every work Christ's Body performs.

That distinction can prevent both complacency and unnecessary guilt.

4. CHRIST IS THE MODEL

Before asking:

“What should I do?”

it helps to ask:

“What was Jesus like?”

Jesus:

  • loved the Father,
  • prayed,
  • worshiped,
  • obeyed,
  • served,
  • taught,
  • listened,
  • healed,
  • forgave,
  • encouraged,
  • corrected,
  • sacrificed,
  • welcomed,
  • suffered patiently,
  • defended truth,
  • showed mercy,
  • noticed forgotten people,
  • spent time with sinners without approving sin,
  • cared for physical needs,
  • cared for spiritual needs,
  • withdrew for prayer,
  • accepted interruptions,
  • endured misunderstanding,
  • washed His disciples' feet,
  • carried His Cross,
  • gave His life for others.

Christian discipleship means allowing these characteristics of Christ gradually to appear in our own circumstances.

5. LOVE GOD FIRST

Jesus' life was completely centered upon the Father.

Therefore becoming Christlike begins not primarily with doing more things, but with loving God.

This includes:

  • prayer,
  • worship,
  • Mass,
  • the Eucharist,
  • Confession,
  • Scripture,
  • thanksgiving,
  • obedience,
  • trust,
  • surrender,
  • seeking God's will,
  • remembering God's presence.

The greatest commandment remains:

Love God with your whole heart, soul, mind and strength.

Our service of others should flow from this relationship.

Otherwise even good works can become merely activism.

6. LOVE THE PERSON GOD HAS PUT IN FRONT OF YOU

We can imagine Christian service as something dramatic.

Often it isn't.

Sometimes Christ's work for us today is simply:

  • listen patiently to your spouse,
  • call someone who is lonely,
  • help a neighbor,
  • forgive an irritation,
  • play with a grandchild,
  • make supper,
  • drive someone to an appointment,
  • speak kindly,
  • repair something for somebody,
  • pray for a troubled friend,
  • refrain from saying something hurtful,
  • encourage someone who feels discouraged.

We can spend considerable time wondering:

“What great work does God want me to accomplish?”

while overlooking the person He has already placed beside us.

7. HOW JESUS TREATED PEOPLE

To act like Christ means learning His way of seeing people.

Jesus noticed people others ignored.

Therefore:

Notice people.

Jesus listened.

Listen.

Jesus had compassion.

Allow another person's suffering to matter to you.

Jesus forgave.

Forgive.

Jesus spoke truth.

Do not confuse love with pretending everything is right.

Jesus was patient with weakness.

Be patient with imperfect people.

Jesus called sinners to conversion.

Genuine mercy wants another person's ultimate good.

Jesus welcomed children.

Give attention to those who may seem unimportant.

Jesus touched lepers.

Do not avoid people merely because society considers them undesirable.

Jesus wept with those who mourned.

Sometimes presence is more important than advice.

8. THE CORPORAL WORKS OF MERCY

The traditional Corporal Works of Mercy give us concrete ways to serve Christ in people's physical needs.

They are:

  1. Feed the hungry
  2. Give drink to the thirsty
  3. Clothe the naked
  4. Shelter the homeless
  5. Visit the sick
  6. Visit the imprisoned
  7. Bury the dead

These can take many forms.

Feeding the hungry may mean:

  • supporting a food pantry,
  • taking a meal to a family,
  • cooking for someone who is sick,
  • helping a struggling family financially.

Visiting the sick might mean:

  • hospital visits,
  • phone calls,
  • transportation,
  • sitting with someone,
  • bringing Communion if properly commissioned,
  • helping a caregiver,
  • praying for the sick.

You are not necessarily called to perform every possible expression of every work.

Ask:

Which needs has God actually placed within my reach?

9. THE SPIRITUAL WORKS OF MERCY

The Spiritual Works of Mercy are:

  1. Instruct the ignorant
  2. Counsel the doubtful
  3. Admonish sinners
  4. Bear wrongs patiently
  5. Forgive offenses willingly
  6. Comfort the afflicted
  7. Pray for the living and the dead

Notice how different these are.

Someone who cannot perform physically demanding service can still participate profoundly in Christ's work.

An elderly or homebound Catholic may exercise an extraordinary ministry through:

  • prayer,
  • offering suffering,
  • encouragement,
  • forgiveness,
  • telephone calls,
  • spiritual counsel,
  • intercession.

There is no retirement from membership in Christ's Body.

The form of our service may change.

Our membership does not.

10. CHRIST IN THE FAMILY

For most Christians, some of their most important opportunities to imitate Christ occur at home.

This includes:

  • loving one's spouse,
  • remaining faithful,
  • forgiving quickly,
  • raising children,
  • teaching the Faith,
  • caring for aging parents,
  • helping siblings,
  • being patient with family weaknesses,
  • providing materially,
  • listening,
  • praying together,
  • keeping promises,
  • creating peace in the home.

These acts can appear ordinary.

They are not spiritually insignificant.

A person can spend years looking for a “ministry” while overlooking an enormous ministry already entrusted to him:

his family.

11. CHRIST IN ORDINARY DAILY DUTY

Not every Christlike act feels religious.

Sometimes following Christ means:

  • getting out of bed when you should,
  • doing your work properly,
  • paying bills honestly,
  • keeping your word,
  • cleaning the house,
  • repairing what needs repairing,
  • arriving when you said you would,
  • helping without being asked,
  • completing an unpleasant responsibility,
  • being courteous,
  • doing today's duty rather than dreaming about tomorrow's heroic deed.

Ordinary duty performed faithfully and lovingly can become an offering to God.

Holiness is frequently hidden inside ordinary life.

12. CHRIST TOWARD THE POOR AND FORGOTTEN

Jesus repeatedly noticed those society overlooked.

Christians should therefore develop eyes for:

  • the poor,
  • the elderly,
  • the lonely,
  • the disabled,
  • the grieving,
  • the rejected,
  • immigrants and strangers,
  • prisoners,
  • unborn children,
  • struggling families,
  • people without friends,
  • those carrying burdens nobody else sees.

But again:

You personally cannot solve every human problem.

The question is not:

“Why am I not helping everyone?”

The better question is:

“Whom has God placed within my ability to help?”

13. CHRIST TOWARD DIFFICULT PEOPLE

One of the hardest forms of imitation of Christ is loving people we do not naturally like.

Christ calls us to:

  • refrain from revenge,
  • forgive,
  • pray for enemies,
  • refuse hatred,
  • speak truth without cruelty,
  • bear some faults patiently,
  • avoid gossip,
  • refuse unnecessary arguments,
  • seek reconciliation when appropriate.

Love does not necessarily mean:

  • agreeing,
  • trusting an untrustworthy person,
  • permitting abuse,
  • eliminating legitimate boundaries,
  • pretending wrongdoing is acceptable.

Christian love seeks the true good of the other person.

Sometimes that requires tenderness.

Sometimes it requires correction.

Sometimes it requires distance.

Always it requires charity.

14. CHRIST THROUGH PRAYER

Prayer itself is a service to the Mystical Body.

You can pray for:

  • family,
  • friends,
  • priests,
  • bishops,
  • the Pope,
  • government leaders,
  • the sick,
  • sinners,
  • people approaching death,
  • people who have nobody praying for them,
  • persecuted Christians,
  • enemies,
  • the souls in Purgatory,
  • people whose suffering you hear about.

A hidden intercessor may contribute enormously to the Church while receiving almost no recognition on earth.

God sees hidden service.

15. CHRIST THROUGH SUFFERING

Suffering is not good simply because it hurts.

Christianity does not teach us to seek misery for its own sake.

But suffering that cannot reasonably be removed can be united with Christ's suffering.

Illness.

Pain.

Anxiety.

Disappointment.

Aging.

Loss.

Loneliness.

Frustration.

Unwanted limitations.

These can be offered to God in union with Christ for ourselves and others.

This connects directly with the Christian practice of “offering it up.”

Even circumstances that seem to prevent us from serving can themselves become a form of service when lovingly united to Christ.

16. CHRIST THROUGH WORDS

Our speech can become an instrument of Christ.

We can:

  • encourage,
  • teach,
  • comfort,
  • thank,
  • praise what is good,
  • defend someone unfairly criticized,
  • apologize,
  • forgive,
  • give wise advice,
  • speak truth charitably,
  • tell someone that we love them,
  • speak about God when appropriate.

We can also imitate Christ by not speaking.

Sometimes holiness means refusing to:

  • gossip,
  • ridicule,
  • complain unnecessarily,
  • repeat damaging information,
  • insult,
  • exaggerate,
  • provoke an argument.

17. CHRIST THROUGH HIDDEN SERVICE

Some of the most Christlike actions may never be noticed.

Cleaning something nobody knows you cleaned.

Praying for someone who never knows.

Quietly giving money.

Doing another person's chore.

Taking an unpleasant job.

Forgiving without announcing it.

Remaining patient.

Offering suffering.

Making someone's life easier.

Jesus taught us that the Father sees what is done in secret.

Christian service does not require an audience.

18. DIFFERENT GIFTS FOR DIFFERENT MEMBERS

This deserves special emphasis.

God does not create interchangeable Christians.

One person is naturally good at listening.

Another teaches well.

Another organizes.

Another fixes things.

Another makes people feel welcome.

Another gives generously.

Another notices people who are hurting.

Another understands children.

Another cares tenderly for the sick.

Another has courage to defend truth.

Another spends long periods in prayer.

Another can make discouraged people laugh.

Another patiently performs unnoticed work.

Another has intellectual gifts.

Another has artistic gifts.

Another has practical skills.

All can serve Christ.

Do not despise your gift because somebody else's looks more impressive.

And:

Do not despise another person's gift because it doesn't look like yours.

The Body needs different members.

19. YOUR STATE IN LIFE MATTERS

God's will is often discovered through responsibilities that already exist.

Ask:

Who am I?

Am I:

  • married?
  • single?
  • parent?
  • grandparent?
  • caregiver?
  • employee?
  • employer?
  • retired?
  • student?
  • priest?
  • religious?
  • widowed?
  • homebound?

Each situation creates particular opportunities and obligations.

A married person's first responsibility cannot ordinarily be neglected because he wants to perform some impressive outside ministry.

Likewise, caring faithfully for an aging spouse may be a tremendous participation in Christ's sacrificial love.

God often reveals our mission through the people and duties already entrusted to us.

20. AGE AND LIMITATIONS CHANGE THE WORK — NOT THE CALL

What Christ asks at age 25 may be very different from what He asks at 75.

At one stage of life a person might serve through:

  • physical labor,
  • raising children,
  • employment,
  • parish activities.

Later that same person's service might become:

  • encouragement,
  • wisdom,
  • prayer,
  • generosity,
  • helping grandchildren,
  • supporting younger families,
  • offering illness,
  • patiently accepting limitations.

Do not judge today's vocation by yesterday's abilities.

Ask instead:

Lord, given who I am and what I can do today, how do You want to love through me?

21. WHAT YOU ARE NOT EXPECTED TO DO

You are not expected to:

  • solve every problem,
  • help every person,
  • join every ministry,
  • volunteer for everything,
  • possess every charism,
  • eliminate all suffering,
  • say yes to every request,
  • exhaust yourself,
  • imitate another person's vocation,
  • feel guilty because another person does something you do not,
  • accomplish what is genuinely beyond your ability.

Jesus Himself accepted the limitations of a human life.

He did not personally visit every sick person in the Roman Empire.

Yet He perfectly accomplished the Father's will.

The goal is therefore not:

Do everything.

It is:

Be faithful to what God asks of you.

22. BUT “THAT ISN'T MY GIFT” CAN BECOME AN EXCUSE

There is an opposite danger.

We cannot say:

“Helping people isn't my charism.”

Some things belong to charity itself.

If someone collapses in front of us, we cannot simply decide that assisting injured people is somebody else's vocation.

If our spouse needs help, Christian responsibility cannot ordinarily be avoided because our “gift” is something else.

Therefore distinguish between:

UNIVERSAL CHRISTIAN DUTIES

Love, mercy, forgiveness, prayer, honesty, justice, obedience to God and reasonable assistance to neighbors in need.

and:

PARTICULAR CALLINGS

The special ways God asks an individual to serve according to vocation, gifts, opportunities and circumstances.

23. DON'T COMPARE MISSIONS

Comparison can cause trouble in both directions.

We may think:

“She does far more than I do. I must be a poor Christian.”

Or:

“I do much more than he does. He must not care.”

We usually don't know what God is asking of another person.

Someone whose visible service seems small may be:

  • caring for a difficult family member,
  • enduring illness,
  • praying intensely,
  • carrying private sorrow,
  • quietly giving generously,
  • struggling faithfully with limitations nobody else knows.

God judges faithfulness with perfect knowledge.

We don't.

24. HOW DO I DISCOVER MY PART?

Start with what is already clear.

Ask yourself:

1. What duties has God already given me?

Family, work, promises, responsibilities.

2. Who has God placed around me?

These people are probably more important than imaginary people somewhere else.

3. What needs do I repeatedly notice?

Sometimes noticing something others overlook indicates an area in which God may be drawing us.

4. What abilities has God given me?

Practical, intellectual, relational, artistic, financial, organizational, spiritual.

5. What good work repeatedly attracts me?

Not every attraction is a calling, but persistent holy desires deserve attention.

6. What opportunities has Providence placed before me?

Sometimes the mission simply arrives at the door.

7. What can I realistically do?

God does not ask us to pretend we have abilities or resources we do not possess.

8. What produces good spiritual fruit?

Does the activity help you love God and neighbor more faithfully?

9. Have I prayed about it?

Ask the Holy Spirit.

10. For major decisions, have I sought wise counsel?

A priest, spiritual director or mature Catholic can sometimes help distinguish enthusiasm from genuine calling.

25. A SIMPLE DISCERNMENT FORMULA

When wondering whether God may be asking you to do something, consider:

DUTY

Is this already part of my responsibility?

NEED

Is there a genuine need?

ABILITY

Can I reasonably help?

OPPORTUNITY

Has God provided an opening?

DESIRE

Do I experience a persistent good desire toward it?

PRAYER

What happens when I bring it before God?

FRUIT

Does it lead toward greater faith, hope, charity and holiness?

WISDOM

Would prudent Christian counsel support it?

These together can help reveal our place within the Body.

26. SOMETIMES CHRIST SENDS YOU AN INTERRUPTION

We naturally make plans.

Then someone calls.

Someone needs help.

A child interrupts.

A neighbor appears.

Someone needs to talk.

Plans change.

Not every interruption comes from God.

Some should legitimately be declined.

But sometimes the interruption is today's assignment.

Jesus' earthly ministry contained many apparent interruptions.

Learning to ask:

“Lord, is this person an interruption—or is this whom You are giving me right now?”

can change how we see ordinary life.

27. THE PERSON IN FRONT OF YOU MAY BE CHRIST'S ASSIGNMENT

Jesus gives an extraordinary principle in Matthew 25.

What we do for “the least” of His brethren, we do for Him.

This changes Christian service.

We don't merely help someone for Jesus.

In a mysterious way, we encounter Jesus in the person who needs our love.

The hungry person.

The lonely person.

The sick person.

The troublesome person.

The forgotten person.

The family member who needs patience.

Therefore ask:

If I recognized Christ standing before me in this person, would I treat him differently?

28. RECEIVING IS ALSO PART OF THE BODY

This is easily overlooked.

Sometimes we are the person giving.

Sometimes we are the person receiving.

A body requires both.

Illness, aging or misfortune may force someone who has always been independent to accept:

  • meals,
  • transportation,
  • assistance,
  • financial help,
  • companionship,
  • care.

Receiving graciously allows another member of Christ's Body to exercise his gift.

Humility sometimes means helping.

Humility sometimes means allowing yourself to be helped.

29. WHEN YOU FAIL

No member of the Mystical Body except Mary is without sin.

We will sometimes:

  • become impatient,
  • overlook someone,
  • speak harshly,
  • refuse a good opportunity,
  • act selfishly,
  • neglect prayer,
  • fail to forgive quickly.

Christian life does not mean never falling.

When we fail:

  1. Recognize it.
  2. Admit it honestly.
  3. Ask God's forgiveness.
  4. Go to Confession when appropriate.
  5. Repair the harm if possible.
  6. Begin again.

Christ can even use our failures to teach us humility and dependence upon grace.

30. YOU NEED CHRIST TO BECOME LIKE CHRIST

Christianity is not simply self-improvement.

We cannot manufacture holiness by determination alone.

Jesus tells us:

“Apart from me you can do nothing.”
— John 15:5

Therefore remain connected to the Head of the Body through:

  • the Eucharist,
  • Confession,
  • prayer,
  • Scripture,
  • grace,
  • obedience,
  • the life of the Church.

We do not become Christlike merely by trying harder.

We become Christlike because Christ lives in us and transforms us by grace, while we freely cooperate with Him.

31. MARY — THE PERFECT DISCIPLE

Mary gives us an extraordinary model for membership in Christ's Body.

Her response to God was essentially:

“Let it be to me according to your word.”

She:

  • listened,
  • trusted,
  • obeyed,
  • served,
  • pondered,
  • interceded,
  • remained faithful,
  • suffered with Christ,
  • stayed near Him at the Cross.

Mary did not perform every possible apostolic work.

She faithfully performed the mission God gave her.

That is an important lesson.

Holiness is not doing everything.

Holiness is giving ourselves completely to God in the place He gives us.

32. THE SAINTS SHOW THE BEAUTIFUL VARIETY OF THE BODY

Consider how different the saints are.

St. Thérèse of Lisieux emphasized little acts performed with great love.

St. Vincent de Paul served the poor.

St. Thomas Aquinas served through extraordinary intellectual gifts.

St. Francis of Assisi embraced radical poverty.

St. Monica persevered in prayer for her family.

St. Joseph served through work, fatherhood, obedience and quiet faithfulness.

St. Teresa of Calcutta cared for the dying poor.

St. Padre Pio served particularly through prayer, suffering, Confession and spiritual counsel.

Their lives look remarkably different.

Yet each life reveals something of Christ.

No individual saint exhausts the richness of Christ.

Together, the saints give us a much fuller picture.

33. A DAILY “MYSTICAL BODY” EXAMINATION

At the beginning of the day, ask:

MORNING

Jesus, whom have You entrusted to me today?

Where are my responsibilities today?

Is someone likely to need my patience?

Is there someone I should encourage?

Is there someone I should pray for?

What ordinary duty can I perform with love?

Holy Spirit, show me opportunities I might otherwise miss.

At the end of the day, ask:

EVENING

Where did Christ work through me today?

Where did someone show Christ's love to me?

Did I notice the people God placed before me?

Was I patient?

Did I listen?

Did I forgive?

Did I avoid someone I should have helped?

Did I waste energy trying to do something God wasn't asking of me?

Did I accept help graciously?

For what should I thank God?

Where do I need His forgiveness?

Then entrust the entire day to God's mercy.

34. A VERY SHORT RULE FOR DAILY LIFE

When everything in this cheat sheet seems too complicated, remember four things:

LOVE GOD.

DO YOUR DUTY.

LOVE THE PERSON GOD PUTS IN FRONT OF YOU.

RESPOND TO THE GOOD GOD ACTUALLY GIVES YOU THE OPPORTUNITY TO DO.

That alone encompasses an enormous portion of ordinary Christian life.

35. WHEN YOU DON'T KNOW WHAT GOD WANTS

You don't need a special revelation before performing ordinary acts of goodness.

If you don't know your great mission today:

pray.

Do your duty.

Love your family.

Forgive.

Help the person who needs reasonable help.

Perform your work honestly.

Offer your difficulties to God.

Receive the Eucharist faithfully.

Go to Confession.

Be kind.

And remain attentive.

God is perfectly capable of showing you the next step when you need to know it.

36. THE GREAT PICTURE

Imagine the Church throughout the world.

At this moment:

Someone is celebrating Mass.

Someone is feeding a hungry person.

Someone is teaching a child.

Someone is caring for an elderly parent.

Someone is sitting beside a hospital bed.

Someone is hearing a Confession.

Someone is forgiving an enemy.

Someone is building a house.

Someone is comforting a grieving widow.

Someone is raising children.

Someone is quietly praying the Rosary.

Someone is defending an innocent person.

Someone is giving money anonymously.

Someone is cleaning a church.

Someone is studying theology.

Someone is repairing a neighbor's car.

Someone is visiting a prisoner.

Someone is suffering in a hospital and offering that suffering to God.

Someone is simply sitting beside a lonely person.

No one person is doing all these things.

The Body is.

And Christ is its Head.

37. THE CENTRAL TRUTH TO REMEMBER

You are not the entire Body of Christ.

You are a member of it.

Therefore you do not have to do everything.

But neither are you unnecessary.

There are particular people you can love.

Particular prayers you can pray.

Particular burdens you can carry.

Particular gifts you can use.

Particular duties you can fulfill.

Particular suffering you can offer.

Particular acts of mercy you can perform.

Some may be visible.

Many may be completely hidden.

The important question is not:

“Am I doing everything a Christian could possibly do?”

Nor is it:

“Am I doing as much as somebody else?”

The better question is:

“Am I allowing Christ to love God and others through the life He has actually given me?”

38. A PRAYER FOR MY PLACE IN THE MYSTICAL BODY

Lord Jesus Christ, You are the Head and I am a member of Your Body.

Help me become more like You.

Give me Your compassion toward those who suffer, Your patience toward those who are difficult, Your mercy toward sinners, Your love for the Father, Your obedience, Your humility, and Your willingness to serve.

Show me the people You have entrusted to me.

Help me recognize the duties that are mine and peacefully leave to others the duties You have entrusted to them.

Keep me from comparing my service with theirs.

Show me the gifts You have given me and teach me to use them generously.

Help me notice the person who needs me today.

When I can act, give me courage to act.

When I should speak, give me the right words.

When I should listen, make me quiet.

When I should forgive, give me mercy.

When I must suffer, help me unite my suffering with Yours.

When another member of Your Body needs to help me, give me the humility to receive.

And when I do not know what You want, help me faithfully perform the duty nearest at hand.

Jesus, live in me. Love through me. Serve through me. Pray through me. Suffer through me. And use my ordinary life for the good of Your whole Body.

Amen.

41. ONE-SENTENCE CHEAT SHEET

If you remember nothing else, remember this:

Every Christian is called to become like Christ, but each member of Christ's Body is given different circumstances, gifts, duties and opportunities through which Christ's love is expressed; therefore I am not called to do everything, but I am called to love faithfully and generously wherever God has placed me.

FINAL DAILY QUESTION

Instead of asking:

“What should Christians be doing?”

occasionally make the question much more personal:

“Lord Jesus, I am one member of Your Body. What do You want to do through me today?”
