Offering It Up

A Catholic Cheat Sheet for Giving Problems, Suffering, Anxiety, and Daily Difficulties to God

In one sentence:
To "offer it up" means to freely give a suffering, difficulty,
sacrifice, or burden to God, unite it with the sacrifice of Jesus
Christ, and ask Him to use it for some good purpose.

Part I --- What "Offer It Up" Means and How to Do It

1. What Does "Offer It Up" Actually Mean?

Suppose something happens that you would rather not have happen:

  • You are sick.
  • Someone hurts you.
  • You are worried about someone you love.
  • You cannot sleep.
  • Your plans fall apart.
  • You are lonely.
  • You are frustrated.
  • You have to do something unpleasant.
  • You are frightened about the future.
  • You are dealing with pain that you cannot presently remove.

Normally our first reaction is:

"I want this to go away."

There is nothing wrong with that.

Jesus Himself prayed in Gethsemane that the cup might pass from Him.

But the Christian can take another step:

"Lord, since this is here, and since I cannot make it disappear right
now, I don't want it to be wasted. I give it to You."

That is the beginning of offering it up.

2. The Basic Pattern

A simple way to remember it is:

ACCEPT → UNITE → OFFER → TRUST

ACCEPT

Acknowledge what is actually happening.

"Lord, this is hard."

Acceptance does not mean liking it. It simply means: "This is what I
am facing right now."

UNITE

Bring your suffering to Jesus.

"Jesus, I unite this suffering with Your suffering."

Christ has already made the perfect offering of Himself to the Father.
We do not create another sacrifice alongside His. Rather, because we
belong to Christ, our little sacrifices can be joined to His perfect
sacrifice.

OFFER

Give the difficulty to the Father with an intention.

"Father, I offer this to You with Jesus for ______."

You might offer it for your family, someone who is suffering, someone's
conversion, the souls in purgatory, the Church, peace, someone who has
asked for prayer, reparation for sin, someone you have difficulty
loving, or whatever intention God knows needs it most.

TRUST

Then leave the results with God.

"Lord, use this however You wish."

You do not have to understand how God will use it. Offering
something to God is an act of faith.

3. A Very Simple Prayer

Jesus, I offer this to You.
I unite it with Your Cross.
Please use it for ______.
I trust You with it.

Sometimes all you may be able to say is:

Jesus, I offer You this.

God understands the rest.

4. What Are We Really Offering?

We are not necessarily saying, "God, I want this suffering."

Rather:

"God, I give You the suffering that is already here."

You can still try to solve the problem, take medicine, go to the doctor,
ask for help, leave a dangerous situation, correct an injustice, or pray
for the suffering to end.

Offering suffering to God does not mean preserving suffering that
could reasonably be relieved.

It means:

While this cross is mine to carry, I will carry it with Christ
rather than carry it alone.

5. Why Can Our Suffering Have Value?

The answer begins with Jesus. Suffering by itself is not what saves us.
Jesus Christ saves us.

But through Baptism we have been united to Christ. Therefore, the
ordinary things of our lives---including suffering---can be offered
through Him, with Him, and in Him to the Father.

St. Paul gives us the mysterious statement in Colossians 1:24 about
rejoicing in sufferings for the sake of Christ's Body, the Church.

This does not mean Christ's sacrifice was insufficient. Christ's
saving work is perfect. Rather, Christ allows members of His Body to
participate in His suffering and His work of love.

Instead of suffering being merely something happening to me, it can
become something I give to God.

6. The Difference Between Enduring and Offering

Imagine you have a painful headache for four hours.

You can simply endure it, or you can pray:

"Jesus, I don't want this headache, and if it can go away, please take
it away. But while I have it, I offer these next few hours to You for
my family."

The headache may be exactly the same.

But your relationship to it has changed.

It has become an offering of love.

7. You Can Ask God to Remove It AND Offer It

These are not contradictory. Jesus Himself shows us this in Gethsemane.

You can pray:

Father, please take this problem away.
But until You do---or if You choose not to---I offer it to You.
Help me carry it with Jesus.

8. Offering Anxiety to God

You don't need to think, "I shouldn't feel anxious."

Instead, bring the anxiety itself to God:

Jesus, I am afraid.
You know what I am worried about.
I give You this fear.
I offer even the discomfort of this anxiety to You.
Please help me do what I reasonably can, and trust You with what I
cannot control.

Distinguish between two things:

What I can do: Do it.

What I cannot control: Give it to God.

Sometimes you may have to make that offering fifty times in one day.
That does not mean the first prayer failed. Each return to God can
itself become another act of trust.

9. Offering Problems to God

Not every offering involves physical suffering. Unexpected bills, broken
things, canceled plans, computer problems, waiting, mistakes, irritating
people, and delays can become tiny offerings.

Instead of only thinking, "Why does this always happen?" try:

"Lord, I give You this frustration."

Then continue dealing with the problem. This is one way ordinary life
becomes prayer.

10. Offering Something for Another Person

Suppose someone you love has left the Faith:

Jesus, I offer You this pain today for ______. Draw him/her
closer to You.

Or if you wake during the night and cannot sleep:

Lord, I offer this hour for my children and grandchildren.

An unwanted hour has become an hour of intercession.

11. Offering Small Things

Do not wait for great suffering. Opportunities include waiting in line,
traffic, unpleasant chores, tiredness, giving up the better seat,
listening patiently, not answering an irritating remark, getting out of
bed, accepting interruptions, or doing a job carefully when nobody will
notice.

Quietly say:

"For You, Jesus."

12. Voluntary Sacrifices

Sometimes we voluntarily give up something good: a snack, entertainment,
comfort, time, or money.

"Jesus, I offer this little sacrifice for ______."

The sacrifice is not valuable merely because it is unpleasant. Its
deepest value comes from love.

13. The Mass Is the Great School of Offering

At Mass, Christ's one sacrifice is made sacramentally present. We bring
ourselves to that sacrifice.

Our prayers, work, joys, sufferings, sacrifices, worries, family, plans,
and disappointments can all be placed spiritually on the altar.

When the priest says:

"Pray, brethren, that my sacrifice and yours may be acceptable to
God, the almighty Father..."

notice: "my sacrifice and yours."

That "yours" includes the offering of your own life in union with
Christ.

14. A Good Practice Before Mass

Before Mass begins, quietly choose something to place on the altar:

Father, today I place on this altar my worry about ______.
I unite it to the sacrifice of Jesus.
Please receive it and use it according to Your will.

15. The Morning Offering

Father, I offer You today---my prayers, work, joys, difficulties,
sacrifices, and sufferings.
I unite them with Jesus and offer them for Your glory and for the
needs of others.\
Help me receive whatever comes today with faith, love, and trust.

Then when something difficult happens later, remember:

"I already gave God this day."

16. When You Forget to Offer It

If you remember only afterward:

"Lord, I didn't think of You while that was happening, but I give it
to You now."

God is not looking for perfect technique. He is looking for your heart.

17. When You Offer It---and Still Complain

If irritation returns:

"Jesus, I'm struggling again. I give it to You again."

Offering something up is often not one heroic decision. It may be many
little acts of surrender.

18. Offering Suffering Does Not Mean Calling Evil Good

Disease, death, cruelty, injustice, and sin are not good.

The Cross teaches something greater:

God can take something genuinely terrible and bring good from it.

So when Christians offer suffering to God, they express faith that:

No suffering given to Christ in love has to be meaningless.

19. What About Suffering Caused by My Own Mistakes?

First acknowledge your responsibility. If necessary, repent, go to
Confession, apologize, make restitution, and correct what you can.

Then:

"Lord, I caused some of this myself. Forgive me. Help me repair what
I can, and I offer You the suffering that remains."

20. What If I Don't Feel Holy About It?

You can be frightened, tired, upset, or crying. Offering suffering is an
act of the will, not necessarily a pleasant feeling.

"Jesus, I really don't want this---but I give it to You anyway."

21. What If the Suffering Is Too Much?

The prayer can become very small:

Jesus, help me.

Jesus, stay with me.

Jesus, I give this to You.

Or simply:

Jesus.

22. Offering Is Not Bargaining

Offering is not: "If I offer this suffering, God owes me the thing I
requested."

We may ask for a particular intention, but ultimately:

"Father, use this according to Your wisdom."

23. A Practical 30-Second Method

  1. NAME IT: "Lord, this hurts."
  2. ASK FOR HELP: "Please help me with it---and remove it if that is
    Your will."
  3. UNITE IT: "I unite this with Jesus' suffering."
  4. OFFER IT: "I offer it for ______."
  5. SURRENDER THE RESULT: "Use it however You know is best."
  6. DO THE NEXT RIGHT THING: Continue with whatever reasonable
    action is needed.

Offering something to God doesn't replace action. It changes the
spirit in which we act.

24. One Prayer for Almost Any Situation

Father, I give You this difficulty.
If it can be removed, please remove it.
If I must carry it, help me carry it with Jesus.
I unite it with His Cross and offer it to You for ______.
Bring whatever good You desire from it.
Give me the grace to do what I should do,
and to trust You with what I cannot control.
Jesus, I trust in You. Amen.

25. The Shortest Version

Jesus, I give You this.
Join it to Your Cross
and use it for good.

Or:

Jesus, this is for You.

26. The Big Idea

The Catholic answer to suffering is not simply "Put up with it."

It is:

"Give it to Christ."

Jesus can transform pain into offering, waiting into prayer, frustration
into sacrifice, fear into trust, interruption into charity, helplessness
into intercession, and suffering into love.

You may not be able to choose whether a cross enters your life. But with
God's grace, you can choose what you do with it once it arrives.

Part II --- The Catholic Foundation

Scripture, the Catechism, and the Saints on Offering Suffering to God

The practice of "offering it up" grows out of a profound Christian
teaching:

Because we have been united to Jesus Christ, our prayers, work,
sacrifices, and sufferings can be united to His perfect offering to
the Father.

Our suffering does not replace Christ's sacrifice or add something His
sacrifice lacked. Rather, Christ allows us to participate in His life,
His love, His prayer, and even His suffering.

27. Begin With Jesus

In Gethsemane (Luke 22:42), Jesus asks that the cup be removed, yet
entrusts Himself to the Father's will.

This gives us a model:

"Father, please take this away. But if I must go through it, help me
go through it with You."

28. "Take Up Your Cross"

Luke 9:23 tells us to take up our cross daily and follow Christ.

Most crosses are ordinary: tiredness, disappointment, sickness,
difficult people, interruptions, responsibilities, worries, waiting, and
inconveniences.

The question becomes:

Can I carry today's cross with Jesus instead of merely resenting
it?

29. St. Paul's Remarkable Teaching

Colossians 1:24 speaks of Paul's sufferings for the sake of Christ's
Body, the Church.

What is "lacking" is certainly not the saving power of Christ's
sacrifice. Christ's sacrifice is perfect. The mystery is that Christ
permits His members to participate in His work.

This helps explain why we can pray:

"Jesus, I offer this suffering for someone else."

30. We Are Members of Christ's Body

1 Corinthians 12:27 teaches that Christians are members of Christ's
Body.

Through Baptism we are not merely following Jesus from a distance. Our
lives are united with His. This is why Catholics speak of uniting our
suffering with Christ.

31. Offer Your Very Self to God

Romans 12:1 calls Christians to present themselves as a living
sacrifice.

Ultimately we offer ourselves: our body, work, time, health, weakness,
relationships, responsibilities, joys, sorrows, successes, and failures.

32. Even Ordinary Life Can Become an Offering

1 Peter 2:5 speaks of offering spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God
through Jesus Christ.

Our offerings have value because they are united with Him.

33. The Catechism: Our Suffering Can Be United to Christ

CCC 1505 teaches that Christ takes human suffering seriously and
makes our sufferings His own.

CCC 1508 points to grace and strength amid weakness and suffering.

CCC 1521 explains that suffering can acquire a new meaning through
union with Christ's Passion.

That is very close to the theological meaning of "offer it up."

34. The Catechism and the Daily Offering of Our Lives

CCC 901 teaches that the ordinary lives of the faithful---their
works, prayers, family life, daily occupations, relaxation, and
hardships---can become spiritual sacrifices when offered through Jesus
Christ.

Cooking can be offered. Working can be offered. Rest can be offered.
Caring for someone can be offered. Pain and waiting can be offered.

Your ordinary day can become an altar.

35. The Mass Brings All of This Together

At Mass, Christ's one sacrifice is made sacramentally present, and we
participate.

The priest says:

"Pray, brethren, that my sacrifice and yours may be acceptable to
God, the almighty Father."

Your sacrifice includes your life---your prayers, work, family, worries,
suffering, acts of love, and sacrifices---united to Jesus.

36. St. Thérèse and the "Little Way"

St. Thérèse of Lisieux shows that offerings need not be dramatic.
Ordinary annoyances, duties, weakness, difficult personalities, and
unnoticed sacrifices can become acts of love.

Ask:

Can I turn this little difficulty into an act of love?

Sometimes the whole prayer is:

"Jesus, this is for You."

37. St. Faustina --- Suffering Joined to Jesus

A central theme in St. Faustina's spirituality is not suffering for its
own sake, but trust in Jesus while suffering.

The goal is not "How much suffering can I accumulate?" but:

"How deeply can I belong to Jesus?"

A simple Divine Mercy form of the offering is:

"Jesus, I trust in You.
I give You this suffering.
Unite it to Your Passion and use it according to Your merciful
will."

38. St. John Paul II and Redemptive Suffering

In Salvifici Doloris ("On the Christian Meaning of Human Suffering"),
St. John Paul II emphasizes that Christ does not merely explain
suffering from outside it. Christ enters suffering Himself.

The Christian who suffers can therefore discover:

"My suffering is not necessarily useless."

When joined to Christ in love, it can become an offering.

39. Mary Shows Us How to Offer Suffering

At the Annunciation Mary gives her "yes" to God (Luke 1:38). Simeon
later foretells that a sword will pierce her soul (Luke 2:35).
Eventually she stands beneath the Cross.

There are times when we cannot fix what is happening to someone we love.

Mary teaches us:

Sometimes love means remaining faithfully present beneath a cross we
cannot remove.

That presence itself can become an offering.

40. The Crucifix Changes the Meaning of Suffering

Calvary appeared to be catastrophe: rejection, humiliation, abandonment,
suffering, and death.

Yet God brought salvation through the Cross.

This does not make evil good. It reveals:

God can bring good even out of terrible evil.

So we can pray:

"Father, I cannot see what good could possibly come from this. But
Calvary teaches me that You can bring good from things I cannot
understand."

41. Offering Suffering and Trusting God Belong Together

Trust says:

"God, I believe You are with me even though I don't understand
this."

Offering says:

"And because I trust You, I give this to You."

Sometimes the offering itself is:

"Lord, I offer You my inability to understand why this is
happening."

42. A Crucial Catholic Balance

Two errors should be avoided.

Error #1: "Suffering is meaningless, so the only good response is to
eliminate it."

Christianity teaches that suffering can acquire meaning when united with
Christ.

Error #2: "Suffering is holy, so I should never try to relieve it."

Jesus healed the sick. Christians feed the hungry, comfort the
sorrowful, and relieve suffering.

Therefore:

Try to relieve suffering when you can.
Offer to God the suffering you cannot presently remove.

43. Do Not Go Looking for Crosses

Ordinary Christian life will provide enough.

If today brings joy: Thank Him.

If today brings work: Offer it.

If today brings suffering: Unite it to Christ.

If today brings someone who needs you: Love them.

If today brings uncertainty: Trust Him.

44. The Eucharistic Pattern

Jesus takes, blesses, breaks, and gives.

Our lives sometimes feel "broken," but what is placed into Christ's
hands can become a gift.

"Jesus, take this.
I don't know what to do with it.
But You do."

45. A Prayer Before a Crucifix

Jesus, You know what suffering is.
You know this suffering of mine better than I do.
I do not pretend that it is easy,
and I do not understand why You have permitted it.
If it can be removed, I ask You to remove it.
But while it remains, I give it to You.
Unite it with Your suffering on the Cross.
Offer it with Your perfect sacrifice to the Father.
Use it for my good, for those I love,
for the Church, for sinners,
for the souls in purgatory,
and wherever Your mercy knows it is most needed.
Help me not to waste this cross through bitterness.
Help me carry it with You.
And when I cannot carry it well,
carry me.
Jesus, I trust in You. Amen.

46. A Prayer When Someone You Love Is Suffering

Father, I give You my worry and helplessness about ______.
Please help and protect this person.
Give them whatever grace they need.
Show me anything You want me to do for them.
And the part that I cannot fix,
I place into Your hands.
I unite this worry and sorrow with the Cross of Jesus.
Please use even this suffering for good.
Jesus, I trust this person to You.

47. When You Wake Up Worried During the Night

Jesus, You are awake even when the world sleeps.
You know what I am worried about.
I give this person and this problem to You.
I offer You this sleeplessness for ______.
Take care of what I cannot take care of tonight.
Jesus, I trust in You.

If the worry returns: Give it back again.

48. When Pain Suddenly Hits

"Jesus, I offer You this for ______."

Or:

"Jesus, this is Yours."

Or:

"For You, Jesus."

49. A Useful Question to Develop the Habit

Whenever something unpleasant happens, ask:

"Can I give this to Jesus?"

Then ask:

"Who could I offer it for?"

Eventually these questions can become a spiritual habit.

50. A Catholic Formula Worth Remembering

WITH JESUS → TO THE FATHER → FOR LOVE

I unite it WITH JESUS.

I offer it TO THE FATHER.

I ask God to use it FOR LOVE.

54. Final Cheat-Sheet Summary

When suffering comes:

1. FACE IT

"Lord, this is happening."

2. ASK

"Please help me, and remove it if You will."

3. UNITE IT

"Jesus, I unite this with Your Cross."

4. OFFER IT

"Father, I offer this with Jesus for ______."

5. ACT

"Show me what I should do."

6. TRUST

"What I cannot control, I leave with You."

7. REPEAT

Whenever fear, pain, frustration, or worry returns: Offer it again.

The Heart of "Offer It Up"

"Offer it up" does not mean:

"Quit complaining and put up with it."

Properly understood, it means:

"Jesus, something painful has entered my life.
I wish it were different, and I ask for Your help.
But I refuse to let this suffering be meaningless.
I place it in Your hands.
Join it to Your Cross.
Offer it with Yourself to the Father.
Use it for love.
Show me what I should do,
and help me trust You with everything I cannot change."

And when even that is too much to remember:

WITH JESUS → TO THE FATHER → FOR LOVE

That is "offering it up."
