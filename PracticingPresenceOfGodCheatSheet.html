Practicing the Presence of God

A Catholic Cheat Sheet — Why It Matters and How to Do It

The basic idea:
Practicing the Presence of God means developing the habit of remembering, throughout the day, that God is here, God sees me, God loves me, and I am living my life with Him.

It is not necessary to feel God's presence.

It is primarily an act of faith, attention, love, and relationship.

1. What Does “Practicing the Presence of God” Mean?

God is not merely someone we visit during prayer.

He is present throughout our entire lives.

Practicing His presence means learning to live with an interior awareness:

I am never alone. God is here.

It means gradually bringing God into:

  • prayer
  • work
  • meals
  • conversations
  • chores
  • decisions
  • recreation
  • suffering
  • waiting
  • driving
  • frustrations
  • relationships
  • quiet moments
  • ordinary duties

Instead of dividing life into:

“time with God”
and
“the rest of my life,”

we gradually learn to live the whole day with God.

2. Why Practice the Presence of God?

Because Christianity is ultimately not merely about following rules.

It is about living in relationship with God.

Jesus says:

“Abide in me, and I in you.”
— John 15:4

Practicing God's presence helps turn this from an idea into a way of life.

Over time it can help us:

  • pray more naturally
  • trust God more deeply
  • resist temptation
  • become more patient
  • recognize God's blessings
  • accept difficulties more peacefully
  • make better decisions
  • remember our dependence upon God
  • offer our work to Him
  • see other people differently
  • grow in gratitude
  • become less self-centered
  • unite ordinary life with prayer

The goal is not simply:

“Think about God more often.”

The deeper goal is:

“Live your life with God.”

3. God Is Already Present

Practicing God's presence does not cause God to become present.

He already is.

We are learning to become aware of a reality that already exists.

God is present to all creation because everything depends upon Him for its existence.

But for a Christian living in grace there is also an especially intimate reality.

Jesus said:

“If a man loves me, he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.”
— John 14:23

Catholic tradition speaks of this as the indwelling of the Holy Trinity in the soul in the state of grace.

So practicing God's presence can include remembering:

The Father loves me.
Christ is with me.
The Holy Spirit dwells within me.
I belong to the Holy Trinity.

4. The Eucharist Gives This Practice a Center

God is present everywhere, but the Catholic faith teaches that Christ is present in a unique and supreme way in the Most Holy Eucharist — Body, Blood, Soul and Divinity.

The Eucharist therefore becomes the great center of the Christian's life with God.

Practicing God's presence throughout the day can flow naturally from the Mass:

At Mass:
I encounter Christ sacramentally.

After Mass:
I carry my relationship with Christ into ordinary life.

Throughout the day:
I remember Him.

Before the tabernacle:
I return to Him in His Eucharistic Presence.

This makes ordinary life and Eucharistic worship parts of one relationship.

5. The Simplest Method

You do not need a complicated spiritual technique.

Several times during the day, simply stop interiorly for a few seconds and remember:

God is here.

Then speak to Him.

For example:

Jesus, I love You.

Lord, I trust You.

Father, thank You.

Holy Spirit, guide me.

Jesus, help me with this.

Lord, I offer this to You.

Thank You for being with me.

Then continue whatever you were doing.

It may take only five or ten seconds.

That counts.

6. Turn Ordinary Actions into Reminders of God

Ordinary events can become little bells calling your attention back to God.

When You Wake Up

Before thinking about the day's problems:

Good morning, Lord. Thank You for another day. I give this day to You.

Before Beginning Work

Lord, help me do this well and for Your glory.

When Something Goes Well

Thank You, Lord.

When Something Goes Wrong

Jesus, I trust You. Help me handle this well.

When You See Something Beautiful

Thank You for making this, Lord.

When Someone Irritates You

Lord, help me see this person as You see them.

When You Must Make a Decision

Holy Spirit, guide me.

When You Are Worried

Father, You know what I need. I place this in Your hands.

When You Are Suffering

Jesus, I unite this suffering with Yours and offer it to You.

Before Going to Sleep

Thank You for staying with me today. Forgive my failures. I place tomorrow in Your hands.

7. Use “Triggers”

One of the easiest ways to develop the habit is to connect remembrance of God with things you already do.

For example:

Every time I...

  • wake up → thank God
  • eat → thank God
  • enter the car → ask for protection
  • hear a church bell → remember God
  • see a crucifix → tell Jesus I love Him
  • begin work → offer it to God
  • become irritated → ask for patience
  • become worried → make an act of trust
  • see someone suffering → pray for that person
  • finish something → thank God
  • go to bed → review the day with God

Eventually ordinary life itself begins reminding us of God.

8. Talk to God During the Day

Prayer does not always have to be formal.

God can be addressed simply and naturally.

Tell Him:

  • what you are thinking
  • what worries you
  • what made you happy
  • what you don't understand
  • what you hope for
  • where you failed
  • what you need
  • whom you are concerned about

You might simply say:

Lord, You know what I'm thinking about right now. Help me with it.

This is prayer.

The Catechism describes prayer beautifully:

“Prayer is the life of the new heart.”
— CCC 2697

The Christian life itself can increasingly become prayer.

9. Silence Is Also Prayer

You don't always need to say something.

Sometimes simply recognize:

God is here, and I am with Him.

Rest there for a moment.

There can be great value in simply looking toward God interiorly without producing many words.

Think of two people who love one another sitting quietly together.

Their silence does not mean nothing is happening.

Their presence to one another is itself meaningful.

10. Bring Your Work to God

Brother Lawrence, famous for teaching the practice of God's presence, discovered that ordinary kitchen work could become prayer.

That principle applies almost anywhere.

You can pray while:

  • washing dishes
  • repairing something
  • mowing grass
  • cleaning
  • cooking
  • gardening
  • working at a computer
  • caring for someone
  • driving
  • doing paperwork
  • performing unpleasant chores

Before beginning:

Lord, I offer this work to You. Help me do it well.

Then do the work.

You don't have to keep consciously thinking about God every second.

Doing your duty well for God is itself an act of love.

11. Your Daily Duty Can Become Prayer

This is extremely important.

Practicing God's presence does not mean constantly interrupting your responsibilities to say prayers.

Sometimes the most loving thing you can do for God is to give your full attention to the duty He has placed before you.

If you are:

  • caring for your family
  • listening to someone
  • repairing something
  • preparing food
  • doing your job
  • driving safely
  • helping a neighbor

then concentrating on that responsibility may itself be part of living in God's presence.

You can begin the task with:

For You, Lord.

And then give the task your attention.

12. Practice God's Presence in Other People

Jesus gives this practice another dimension.

He identifies Himself particularly with those in need:

“As you did it to one of the least of these my brethren, you did it to me.”
— Matthew 25:40

Therefore practicing God's presence does not mean withdrawing into ourselves.

Sometimes we encounter Christ precisely by turning our attention away from ourselves and toward another person.

Ask:

How would Christ want me to treat this person?

That question alone can transform:

  • marriage
  • family life
  • friendships
  • disagreements
  • caregiving
  • encounters with strangers

13. Practice His Presence During Suffering

Suffering often makes God's presence hardest to recognize.

At those moments, practicing His presence may simply mean refusing to believe that He has abandoned us.

You might pray:

Lord, I don't understand this, but I believe You are here.

Or:

Jesus, stay with me in this.

Or:

Father, I place this into Your hands.

You do not have to pretend suffering is pleasant.

You do not have to understand why God permits it.

Practicing God's presence during suffering means believing:

I am suffering — but I am not suffering alone.

14. What If You Don't Feel God's Presence?

This is one of the most important lessons.

God's presence is not a feeling.

Sometimes prayer feels:

  • peaceful
  • warm
  • consoling
  • intimate

Other times it feels:

  • dry
  • distracted
  • empty
  • ordinary
  • difficult

God has not necessarily become more present in the first case or less present in the second.

Faith says:

God is here whether I feel Him or not.

In fact, quietly turning toward God when you feel nothing can be a profound act of faith.

15. What About Distractions?

You will forget God.

Probably many times.

That is normal.

The solution is not frustration.

Simply return.

You suddenly realize:

“I haven't thought about God for three hours.”

Don't say:

“I failed again.”

Instead say:

“Lord, I'm back.”

And continue.

Every return to God is itself an act of love.

The practice is largely made up of thousands of little returns.

16. What About Sin and Failure?

When you sin, don't hide from God.

Turn toward Him.

Say:

Lord, I did wrong. I'm sorry. Help me begin again.

Serious sin should lead a Catholic to the Sacrament of Reconciliation.

But even our failures can become occasions for:

  • humility
  • repentance
  • trust
  • renewed dependence upon God

The devil would like failure to make us run away from God.

Christian repentance makes us run toward Him.

17. Short Prayers Are Extremely Useful

Catholic tradition has long valued short prayers sometimes called aspirations or ejaculatory prayers.

Examples:

Jesus, I trust in You.

Jesus, I love You.

My Jesus, mercy.

Sacred Heart of Jesus, I trust in You.

Come, Holy Spirit.

Lord, increase my faith.

Father, into Your hands.

Jesus, meek and humble of heart, make my heart like Yours.

Lord, help me.

Lord, thank You.

Lord, I offer this to You.

Thy will be done.

These prayers can gradually become almost instinctive.

18. Use Physical Reminders

Catholic life is wonderfully suited to this because it is sacramental and physical.

Useful reminders include:

  • crucifixes
  • holy pictures
  • statues
  • rosaries
  • medals
  • holy water
  • church bells
  • candles
  • Scripture
  • religious artwork

The object is not God.

Rather, the object turns our attention toward God.

A crucifix on the wall can silently remind us:

Christ loved me and gave Himself for me.

19. Don't Try to Manufacture a Spiritual Feeling

A common mistake would be continually asking:

“Do I feel God's presence right now?”

That can make the practice excessively self-focused.

A better question is:

“How can I love God right now?”

Sometimes the answer is prayer.

Sometimes gratitude.

Sometimes patience.

Sometimes accepting an inconvenience.

Sometimes doing your work.

Sometimes helping someone.

Sometimes simply saying:

Jesus, I trust You.

20. Don't Make the Practice Anxious

You are not required to consciously think about God every second.

That would make normal human life almost impossible.

Think instead of a loving relationship.

A husband does not consciously think about his wife every second of the day in order to love her.

Yet the relationship remains part of his life.

Similarly, practicing God's presence means developing an underlying orientation:

My life belongs to God, and I am living this day with Him.

21. A Very Simple Daily Practice

Morning

Before getting out of bed:

Father, thank You for this day. I offer it to You. Help me live it with You.

During the Day

Occasionally pause for five seconds:

God is here.

Then perhaps:

Jesus, I trust You.

Before Important Tasks

Lord, help me do this well.

When Something Good Happens

Thank You, Lord.

When Something Difficult Happens

Jesus, help me carry this with You.

When You Notice Someone in Need

Lord, bless this person. Show me whether I should help.

Evening

Ask:

Where did I notice God today?

Where did I forget Him?

What am I thankful for?

Where did I fail to love?

Then:

Thank You, Lord. Forgive me where I failed. I place everything in Your hands.

22. A More Advanced Practice

As the habit develops, try occasionally asking:

Lord, what are You asking of me right now?

Often the answer will be surprisingly ordinary:

Be patient.

Listen.

Finish your work.

Forgive.

Stop complaining.

Help someone.

Rest.

Pray.

Keep your promise.

Accept the inconvenience.

Do the next duty well.

God's will is frequently found not in extraordinary experiences but in faithfully loving Him through the ordinary duties of the present moment.

23. The Connection with Trust

Practicing God's presence and trusting God naturally reinforce one another.

When something goes wrong:

Presence says:

God is here.

Trust says:

Therefore I don't face this alone.

Surrender says:

I place what I cannot control into His hands.

Love says:

Show me what You want me to do now.

This creates a powerful little spiritual sequence:

Remember → Trust → Surrender → Act

24. The Connection with “Offering It Up”

The practice of God's presence also makes offering things to God more natural.

Instead of waiting until later to remember:

“I should have offered that suffering up,”

we learn to turn toward God while the difficulty is happening:

Jesus, You are here. I unite this difficulty to Your Cross and offer it to You. Help me bear it with love.

Thus practicing God's presence, trusting God, and offering things up are not separate spiritual techniques.

They can become different aspects of one continuing relationship with God.

25. The Goal Is Friendship with God

The purpose is not to become skilled at a spiritual exercise.

The purpose is God Himself.

Jesus tells His disciples:

“I have called you friends.”
— John 15:15

The practice of God's presence gradually changes prayer from something we merely do into a relationship we increasingly live.

We begin with:

I should remember God more often.

We gradually move toward:

God is part of everything I do.

And eventually the deepest attitude becomes:

Lord, my whole life is Yours. Let me live every part of it with You.

Quick Reference

When you don't know what else to do, remember these five words:

GOD IS HERE WITH ME.

Then respond according to the situation:

Something good?
→ Thank Him.

Something difficult?
→ Ask His help.

Worried?
→ Trust Him.

Suffering?
→ Offer it to Him.

Tempted?
→ Turn toward Him.

Making a decision?
→ Ask for guidance.

Someone needs you?
→ Love Christ in that person.

Doing ordinary work?
→ Do it well for Him.

Failed?
→ Ask forgiveness and return.

Nothing particular happening?
→ Simply enjoy being with Him.

One-Minute Version

If you remember nothing else:

1. Remember

God is here.

2. Speak

Lord, I love You. Help me.

3. Offer

I give You what I am doing right now.

4. Trust

Whatever happens, stay with me and help me respond well.

5. Return

Whenever you forget God, simply begin again.

Final Thought

Practicing the Presence of God is not about achieving constant religious concentration.

It is about developing a habitual friendship with God.

You will forget Him.

Return.

You will become distracted.

Return.

You will fail.

Return.

You will sometimes feel close to Him and sometimes feel nothing.

Return.

Again and again:

God is here.
God loves me.
I belong to Him.
I can speak to Him now.
I can do this next thing with Him.

That repeated turning toward God can gradually transform an ordinary day into a life of prayer.
