Relationship With Jesus

Only in this personal relationship with Christ, only in this encounter with the Risen One do we truly become Christian - Pope Benedict XVI

What Does This Relationship with Jesus Look Like?

A relationship with Jesus is not complicated, although growing in that relationship takes a lifetime. It means living each day knowing that Jesus loves you, is present to you, and wants you to live in friendship with Him. You learn to trust Him, speak to Him, listen to His word, follow His teachings, and include Him in your daily life. Let's look at what that means in practical terms.

1. You become aware of His presence

Jesus is not merely someone who lived 2,000 years ago. He is alive, and through Baptism, you have been brought into a relationship with Him. Jesus says: "Abide in me, and I in you." — John 15:4. Notice the words in me and I in you. Jesus is not asking you merely to know about Him. He wants you to share in His life. St. Paul writes: "It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me." — Galatians 2:20.

The Catechism teaches that Baptism unites us to Christ and makes us members of His Body, the Church (CCC 1267). What does this look like in everyday life? You might begin the morning with this simple thought: Jesus, You are with me. Help me live this day with You. You don't have to feel His presence. You simply believe what He has revealed and remind yourself of this reality throughout the day.

2. You talk to Him throughout the day

Think about a good friendship. You don't speak to your friend only when you need something. You share your thoughts, concerns, joys, and disappointments. Your relationship with Jesus can develop in a similar way. For example:

  • In the morning: "Jesus, thank You for another day."
  • When facing a problem: "Jesus, help me know what to do."
  • When something good happens: "Jesus, thank You for that."
  • When you are worried: "Jesus, I place this in Your hands."
  • When you make a mistake: "Jesus, have mercy on me and help me do better."
  • When you see someone suffering: "Jesus, help me treat this person with love."

You don't need special words or lengthy prayers.

The Catechism describes prayer as a relationship of the children of God with their Father, with His Son Jesus Christ, and with the Holy Spirit (CCC 2565). Prayer becomes more than something you do at certain times. It becomes part of how you live.

And remember, you don't have to keep talking. Sometimes, simply remembering that Jesus is with you is enough.

3. You learn to listen to Him

A relationship isn't only about talking. It also involves listening. But how do we listen to Jesus? We shouldn't expect to hear an audible voice or receive a direct answer to every question. Instead, we listen through:

  • Scripture: We learn what Jesus teaches and what He asks of us.
  • The Church: We receive guidance through the Church's teachings.
  • Conscience: We recognize the moral demands of doing what is right.
  • Prayerful reflection: We consider how to apply Christ's teachings to our circumstances.
  • Providence: We respond faithfully to the opportunities and responsibilities God permits in our lives.

For example, suppose someone has offended you. You might naturally want to retaliate. But you remember Jesus' teaching: "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you." — Matthew 5:44. You pause, consider His words, and choose not to retaliate.

You have listened to Jesus and responded to Him. You haven't necessarily heard a new message from Him. You've taken His existing teaching to heart and allowed it to guide your actions. Listening to Jesus means being willing to let His words change how you think and live.

4. You trust Him with your life

A relationship with Jesus grows as you learn to trust Him. This doesn't mean believing that everything will happen the way you want. It means believing that He loves you and remaining faithful even when you don't understand your circumstances. Jesus says: "Let not your hearts be troubled; believe in God, believe also in me." — John 14:1.

Imagine that you have a problem you cannot solve immediately. You pray for help, do what you reasonably can, and leave the outcome in God's hands. You might say: "Jesus, I don't know how this will turn out, but I trust You. Help me do what is right and accept what I cannot control." Then you go about your responsibilities.

You don't have to keep worrying about whether you've surrendered the problem sufficiently. You can return to your duties, trusting in God's care. Trust grows through repeated acts of turning to Jesus, especially when life is difficult.

5. You try to please Him because you love Him

A relationship with Jesus isn't simply about feeling close to Him or receiving His help. Love also involves responding to His love. Jesus tells His disciples: "If you love me, you will keep my commandments." — John 14:15. Suppose you are tempted to speak unkindly about someone. You remember that Jesus calls you to love your neighbor. You choose not to participate. Or suppose you realize that you've been impatient with a family member. You acknowledge your fault, ask God's forgiveness, and try to be more patient. These ordinary decisions are important. You aren't trying to earn Jesus' love. You're responding to the love He has already shown you. And when you fail, you don't have to run away from Him in shame. You return to Him, repent, seek His mercy, and begin again. A relationship with Jesus means allowing His love to influence the choices you make, including the small ones nobody else sees.

6. You spend time with Him even when nothing seems to happen

Every relationship needs time. We make time for people we love, and we need time for prayer if our relationship with Jesus is to grow. But prayer doesn't always produce a feeling of closeness. Some days, prayer may be peaceful. Other days, your mind may wander, you may feel distracted, or you may wonder whether anything is happening. That doesn't mean your relationship with Jesus has failed. You can spend a few minutes reading the Gospels, praying the Our Father, reflecting on His words, or simply resting quietly in His presence. You might say: "Jesus, I am here with You. My mind is wandering, but I want to be with You." Then gently return your attention to Him when you notice that you've become distracted.

St. Francis de Sales taught that holiness is possible in ordinary life, through the faithful practice of love in our particular circumstances. You don't need to become a monk or spend hours in prayer to develop a relationship with Jesus. You need to make room for Him in the life you already have.

7. You recognize that Jesus wants more than occasional visits

Consider the difference between these two ways of living. The first approach: "I pray in the morning and evening. During the rest of the day, I take care of things myself. I'll turn to Jesus when I need Him." The second approach: "I pray in the morning and evening, but Jesus is part of my whole day. I turn to Him when I'm happy, when I'm worried, when I need guidance, and when I have nothing particular to say. I try to live according to His teachings and trust Him with the results." The second approach reflects a growing relationship with Jesus.

It doesn't mean praying constantly in a way that prevents you from doing your work. Nor does it mean you must consciously think about Him every moment. It means developing an underlying awareness that you belong to Him, He loves you, and you want to live your life with Him. St. Paul expresses this beautifully: "Pray constantly." — 1 Thessalonians 5:17. This doesn't require uninterrupted verbal prayer. It means cultivating a life that remains oriented toward God. You can work, eat, drive, fix something around the house, or help a family member while still living as a Christian who belongs to Jesus.

8. What does Jesus want from this relationship?

Perhaps the clearest answer is found in Jesus' own words: "As the Father has loved me, so have I loved you; abide in my love." — John 15:9. Jesus wants you to remain in His love. He wants you to know Him, believe in Him, trust Him, love Him, follow Him, and receive the life He offers. And He wants your relationship with Him to bear fruit in how you live. As Jesus explains: "By this my Father is glorified, that you bear much fruit, and so prove to be my disciples." — John 15:8. That fruit includes love, patience, kindness, forgiveness, faithfulness, and self-control (Galatians 5:22–23).

You may not notice dramatic changes from one day to the next. But over time, your relationship with Jesus should help you become more willing to forgive, more faithful in your responsibilities, less controlled by fear, and more attentive to the needs of others. Not perfectly, but increasingly. And when you fall short, you return to Him. That, too, is part of the relationship.

9. A simple way to begin living your relationship with Jesus

You don't need a complicated program. Try these five small practices.

When What to do
Morning Thank Jesus for the new day and ask Him to guide you.
During work Remember occasionally that He is with you.
When facing a problem Ask for His help, do your part, and entrust the outcome to Him.
When you fail Acknowledge your fault, ask forgiveness, and begin again.
Evening Thank Him for the day, reflect briefly on your actions, and entrust yourself to His care.

You can also make a habit of reading a few verses from one of the Gospels each day. Ask yourself two simple questions:

  1. What does this teach me about Jesus?
  2. How can I put this into practice today?

These practices are small, but they help turn your faith into a lived relationship.

A short prayer for the day

Jesus, You are with me, and I belong to You.
Help me remember Your presence, trust Your care, listen to Your word, and follow Your teachings.
May I live this day with You and do all things out of love for You.
Amen.

One final thought

A relationship with Jesus is not primarily about trying to find Him somewhere outside yourself. It begins with receiving Him in faith and living in the reality of your union with Him. Jesus said: "Abide in me, and I in you." — John 15:4. Through Baptism, you have been united to Christ. Through sanctifying grace, God dwells in the soul. Through the Eucharist, Christ gives Himself to us in a unique and sacramental way. These truths are related, but they are not identical. The practical point is that you don't have to spend your day trying to make Jesus come closer. Rather, you can remember His love, turn your attention toward Him, and respond to His presence through faith, prayer, and loving action.

As the Catechism explains, the life of prayer is being habitually in the presence of the thrice-holy God and in communion with Him (CCC 2565). You might sum up your daily relationship with Jesus in three simple thoughts:

  • Remember Him.
  • Trust Him.
  • Respond to Him.

You won't do this perfectly, and you don't need to. When you become distracted, remember Him again. When you become worried, turn to Him again. When you sin, return to Him again. Each return is an opportunity to grow in your relationship with Him.
